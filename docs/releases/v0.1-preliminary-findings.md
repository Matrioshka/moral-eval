## Main findings

The canonical evidence-strength dataset suggests that tested models usually update correctly when safeguards are genuinely strong.

The harder failure mode is weak or irrelevant reassurance: models can over-credit weak procedural safeguards, institutional reputation, managerial assurances, usability polish, affective design, awards, or public praise.

The trap-expansion diagnostic set sharpened this result. It showed that weak procedural safeguards and cosmetic/reputational reassurance are useful probes for moral-reasoning integrity.

The structured relevance-tracking prompt improved performance across the five-model continuity suite on the trap-expansion diagnostic dataset.

Structured prompt comparison:

| Model | Natural context | Structured | Delta |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| gpt-4.1-mini | 34/36 | 36/36 | +2 |
| gemma-3-4b-it | 31/36 | 36/36 | +5 |
| gpt-4o-mini | 30/36 | 32/36 | +2 |
| qwen-2.5-7b-instruct | 27/36 | 32/36 | +5 |
| llama-3.1-8b-instruct | 31/36 | 32/36 | +1 |

The structured prompt helped most on irrelevant reassurance. It helped less on weak procedural safeguards, where the task is not simply rejecting irrelevance but calibrating partial relevance.

## Dataset policy at checkpoint

- Keep `v3_evidence_strength_v1` as the frozen canonical broad calibration dataset.
- Keep `v3_evidence_strength_trap_expansion_v1` as a diagnostic stress test.
- Do not merge diagnostic items into a future canonical v2 without prior selection rules.
- Do not expand datasets merely to increase sample count.

## Known limitations

- Small datasets.
- Manual scoring.
- Limited continuity-suite model set.
- Synthetic scenarios.
- Behavioural evidence only; no mechanistic interpretability claim.
- Prompt-sensitive results; structured prompting may scaffold better outputs without proving robust internal representations.

## Next phase

Phase 2 should be a separate contemporary model suite, not an extension of the original five-model continuity table.

The purpose of Phase 2 is to test whether newer and stronger available models show the same pattern:

- strong evidence is usually handled well;
- irrelevant reassurance is easier to reject under structured prompting;
- weak procedural safeguards remain the hardest calibration problem.
